# Exploratory fusion and mathematical ideas

One-line research questions; preserved identifiers do not establish novelty or feasibility.

## A — Ambitious extensions and supporting mathematics

- **A01 — Polarized D–T:** prepare and preserve useful spin states through injection/compression while accounting for depolarization costs.
- **A02 — Non-Maxwellian fuel:** concentrate useful collision energies while paying for relaxation, radiation, and sustaining power.
- **A03 — Alpha channeling:** route charged-product energy toward recovery or fuel heating without silently sacrificing self-heating.
- **A04 — Entrance-state alignment:** test which spin, orientation, or momentum correlations survive preparation into a nuclear collision.
- **A05 — Channel suppression:** seek physically reachable amplitude interference or selection rules that reduce unwanted channels.
- **A06 — Neutron-direction control:** assess whether prepared nuclear states can usefully alter neutron loading and blanket placement.
- **A07 — Cluster-overlap engineering:** improve a specified entrance-to-compound-state overlap with realizable preparation.
- **A08 — Alternative cluster fuels:** examine proton–boron or other fuel channels with complete loss and preparation accounting.
- **A09 — QCD erasure map:** determine which microscopic information survives into controllable reactor variables.
- **A10 — Transport-spectrum geometry:** tune geometry against explicitly modeled loss/transport modes.
- **A11 — Floquet/RF loss control:** test periodic drive against selected loss modes including its power and instability costs.
- **A12 — Mode-selective feedback:** specify a synthetic-medium response that damps harmful modes within latency and actuator limits.
- **A13 — Sheared-flow branch stability:** calculate the stiffness/flow conditions needed to suppress kink and sausage modes at junctions.
- **A14 — Conserved-flux scale selection:** test whether independent flux constraints stabilize both tube radius and network size.
- **A15 — Small core, full-sized plant:** separate possible fusion-engine miniaturization from unavoidable balance-of-plant scale.

## B — Speculative reactor architectures

- **B01 — Entrance-channel reactor:** organize a fusion event around achievable nuclear-state preparation and its total cost.
- **B02 — Magnetic-null vertex:** test a controlled null/separatrix/current-sheet region as a junction rather than assume it confines.
- **B03 — Rotating-vortex vertex:** investigate flow-supported junctions and their dynamical stability.
- **B04 — Plasmoid-collision vertex:** form a fusion-relevant junction through timed compact-toroid collisions.
- **B05 — Active electromagnetic node:** use controlled fields to sustain a junction with explicit bandwidth and power limits.
- **B06 — Closed theta reactor:** connect two junctions by three plasma-carrying branches and test the complete network.
- **B07 — Tetrahedral/spherical cages:** explore higher-connectivity closed plasma networks after local junction physics is established.
- **B08 — Linked networks:** test interacting theta graphs or compact toroids with explicit cross-coupling and loss accounting.
- **B09 — Periodic trivalent lattices:** examine repeated network cells rather than a single toroidal circulation channel.
- **B10 — Reconfigurable networks:** test nested or changing graph architectures with a physical switching mechanism.
- **B11 — Homology as plumbing:** ask whether distinct cycles can separate fuel, ash, energy, or instability transport.
- **B12 — Pulsed graph cycle:** model formation, compression, fusion, relaxation, and recovery as one repeated operating cycle.
- **B13 — Three incoming streams:** distinguish a three-branch collision geometry from a claim of genuine three-body nuclear fusion.
- **B14 — Field-shaping boundaries:** test superconducting/metamaterial boundaries against real temperature, field, and response limits.
- **B15 — Shells and liquid liners:** investigate conducting/rotating media as dynamic field and compression boundaries.
- **B16 — Secondary-plasma medium:** use a separately modeled sheath/distributed-current plasma to shape the confinement response.
- **B17 — Relaxed genus-2 state:** test whether constrained relaxation itself produces a theta-like flux concentration.

## M — Frontier moonshots

- **M01 — Dual-superconductor Y reactor:** seek a lawful macroscopic confining medium with stable plasma-carrying junction networks.
- **M02 — Plasma as preferred defect:** seek a medium whose favored response restores the plasma channel rather than merely tolerates it.
- **M03 — Type-Y phase:** derive a defect composition rule and energetics that permit or favor stable trivalent junctions.
- **M04 — Programmable vacuum:** realize a synthetic constitutive response using sensors, fields, and feedback with full control costs.
- **M05 — Frustration-selected network:** test whether an independently motivated chiral/frustrated medium prefers a defect network.
- **M06 — Spectral network protection:** calculate whether escape and graph-breakup modes can actually be suppressed.
- **M07 — Molecular nuclear pairing phase:** preserve the proposal as rejected in the stated transfer-barrier model, pending independent source verification.
- **M08 — Transient nuclear coherence:** test whether a realizable many-nucleus preparation remains coherent long enough to affect a reaction.
- **M09 — Junction phase slips:** ask whether a defensible order parameter can influence a compressed reaction through phase locking or frustration.
- **M10 — Heavy-nucleus phase effects:** investigate phase-sensitive superfluid heavy-ion collisions without conflating them with light-fuel molecular pairing.
- **M11 — Amplitude-selecting vertex:** connect a realizable junction control to a nuclear density matrix and measurable channel change.

## C — Categories and the multiscale bridge

- **C01 — Nuclear recoupling:** organize spin channels with representation theory without mistaking recoupling identities for reaction probabilities.
- **C02 — Junction fusion tables:** derive allowed sectors and multiplicities before assigning categorical fusion rules to plasma.
- **C03 — One-form MHD bridge:** identify the precise flux symmetry, boundaries, and constitutive regime that survive the proposed reduction.
- **C04 — Topological plasma waves:** calculate wave-band/interface invariants and separately test their relevance to particle transport.
- **C05 — Process category:** build composable controlled flux histories with complete boundary states and explicit equivalence.
- **C06 — Higher defect category:** test whether physical phases, tubes, and junctions satisfy the proposed higher-categorical axioms.
- **C07 — Reconnection associator:** determine whether reconnection can represent invertible recoupling or only a dissipative process map.
- **C08 — Pentagon versus history:** compare distinct reconnection sequences using physical observables rather than final connectivity alone.
- **C09 — Braiding and helicity:** identify the actual motion algebra and conserved quantities for three-dimensional flux structures.
- **C10 — Non-invertible defects:** construct genuine topological operators rather than calling ordinary dissipative information loss a categorical symmetry.
- **C11 — String-diagram architecture:** generate candidate networks from allowed physical compositions and then test their dynamics.
- **C12 — Confinement-to-nuclear functor:** seek a physically meaningful structure-preserving map from macroscopic operations to nuclear preparation.
